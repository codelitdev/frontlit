# Support and knowledge-base products versus FrontLit’s documentation-plus-support loop

Checked 2026-10-10. Primary pages were fetched that day unless a citation notes an earlier “updated” or “edited” date on the vendor page. Prices and plan gates below are what those pages showed. Where a 2026 secondary write-up disagrees with the vendor page, both are recorded. Claims from before 2025 are marked stale.

FrontLit baseline used for the inferences (local/staging sign-off 2026-10-03; not re-researched on the web): one published docs corpus feeds public site search, a customer chatbot that must cite a passage or hand off, and a help-desk copilot that suggests replies with the same citations. Drafts, unmerged branches, and other teams’ content are excluded. A retrieval miss, negative feedback, or escalation becomes review evidence that a person can turn into a draft or a GitHub proposal. Creating the draft does not auto-resolve the ticket. No autonomous customer replies. Changelog can be indexed; blog cannot. Docs live on the team site at `/docs` with the site theme. No separate help-center product, deflection suite, article CSAT, multi-brand centers, or multilingual publishing. Read / write / publish / manage are split, and docs-only members cannot see raw tickets.

# Authoring, public help center, versioning, localization, SEO, and custom domain

### Takeaway
Zendesk, Intercom, and Help Scout publish a hosted help center that is a separate site from the marketing site. Document360 is a documentation product whose public site can be branded, embedded, and given its own domain, but it is still Document360’s site rather than a `/docs` section of the customer’s CMS. None of the four use Git as the authoring system of record.

### Inferences
- Against the FrontLit baseline, all four products treat the help center as its own property. Help Scout is the most explicit: it will not mount docs at a path on the marketing domain. Document360 is the closest to embedding, via an “embedded help center,” but the corpus still lives in Document360 projects, not in the marketing site’s theme and routes.
- Versioning in these products is article revision history inside the vendor, not branches or pull requests. Intercom’s restore-to-draft and Zendesk’s AI-generated drafts (next section) still publish through the help-center editor.
- Localization is a first-class, priced feature for Intercom (Advanced and above for a multilingual help center) and Document360 (languages are a quote input, with machine translation). Help Scout’s model is “another site per language,” plus a beta AI translate and a third-party widget. FrontLit has no multilingual publishing; that is a real gap versus Intercom and Document360, and a smaller gap versus Help Scout.
- Article CSAT exists as a product in Help Scout (face ratings and a Docs report). Zendesk generative search collects thumbs on the AI answer, not a documented article-CSAT suite in the pages fetched. FrontLit’s lack of article CSAT matches “no CSAT on articles,” and Help Scout is the clearest counterexample.

# How AI answers are grounded, and what is plan-gated

### Takeaway
Intercom Fin is the only one of the four with an official, current statement that customer-visible answers include inline links to public sources, and that those links cannot be turned off. The other three ground answers in approved content, but the pages fetched do not say the customer sees a passage-level citation. All four can answer from more than the public help center. Human handoff is a product behavior for Fin, Help Scout AI Answers, and the Document360 chatbot; Zendesk charges an automated resolution only when the AI resolves without a human, and follow-up from help-center search opens a messaging AI agent that consumes that meter.

### Inferences
- FrontLit’s rule is stricter than Fin’s shipped behavior: Fin must show inline links for public sources and must not show them for private ones, but it does not claim a passage-level citation, it answers in its own words, and it will answer from internal articles, snippets, and files the customer cannot open. FrontLit excludes anything unpublished and requires a citation or a handoff.
- Zendesk’s help-center generative search is the closest public-search analog: permission-aware, derived from the top results, with the articles still listed. It is not documented as a passage citation, and a follow-up turns into a metered AI-agent conversation. Agent copilot features are a different, mostly add-on product, not the same citation path.
- Help Scout’s customer bot is Docs-and-public-URL grounded and hands off by not charging when the customer asks for a human, then disabling itself at a spend cap. That is a real handoff. Citations to the customer were not documented.
- Document360’s assistive search does show numbered source links, including to external pages, which is closer to FrontLit’s public-search citation than Fin’s paraphrased chat answer. The chatbot’s “source article cited” claim is marketing-page copy, not a help-center procedure, so treat the citation behavior of the chatbot as less firmly specified than Eddy search.
- Plan gates that matter for a FrontLit comparison: Zendesk knowledge base and AI agents start at Suite Team on the card (US$55 yearly) despite the FAQ’s “every Support plan” line; Copilot agent assist is US$50 extra or the enterprise bundle. Intercom Fin is on every seat plan but metered, and the gap-to-draft loop is Pro. Help Scout’s customer bot is a US$0.75 meter on paid plans; agent drafts are not on the cheapest paid tier if the secondary plan map is right. Document360 will not sell Eddy or the chatbot from a public price list.

### Gaps

# The feedback loop: tickets and misses into documentation changes

### Takeaway
Intercom is the only product here with a shipped, reviewed loop from failed AI conversations to a draft or a published article, and it is gated on the Pro add-on. Accepting the suggestion updates Fin’s knowledge; it does not close the original conversation. Zendesk can generate up to 40 draft articles from tickets once per brand, and its own docs say this is a poor fit for a help center you already maintain. Help Scout and Document360 expose ratings, unanswered questions, and manual improvements. None of the four open a pull request or treat “create the draft” as resolving the ticket.

### Cited Findings
- Zendesk can build a new help center from recent solved or closed tickets plus a business description, up to 40 draft articles, grouped into a proposed hierarchy, with up to 10 preview regenerations. Tickets must be solved or closed, from web form, email, or messaging, on that brand, and must contain usable public conversation. Internal notes are excluded. Spam and one-off issues are excluded. Output language is the locale you pick, not the ticket language. Articles are saved as drafts for admins and permitted agents to edit before publish. The feature can be used once per brand. Zendesk’s note: if you already have a well-maintained help center, it will not suggest enhancements to the current structure; it creates new categories and sections on top, and may duplicate content. It is not available in trial accounts. Plan badge: all Suites Growth, Professional, Enterprise, or Enterprise Plus, or Support with Knowledge Professional or Enterprise. — [Creating help center content from ticket data](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/9409324793498-Creating-help-center-content-using-ticket-data-and-generative-AI)
- Customers on that article, from Oct 2025 through Feb 2026, say there is no way to generate more articles from later tickets, no way to pick tickets, and no check against the existing 1,000-article corpus. One asked in Feb 2026 how to update the help center after new tickets are closed. Those are customer comments, not a Zendesk roadmap commitment. A Jan 2026 comment asks when a continuous gap-detection engine will ship, which implies it had not shipped then. — same article
- Zendesk generative-search thumbs are retained 90 days to improve the model. The article tells admins to improve answers by editing the knowledge base. It does not create or update an article from a thumbs-down. — [Using generative search](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/8888178335898-Using-generative-search-to-provide-AI-powered-answers-to-search-queries)
- Agents can search help-center content from the ticket context panel and insert it without leaving Agent Workspace (articles edited May 2026). That is agent assist, not a ticket-to-draft pipeline. — [Using help center content in tickets](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/5581313653530-Using-help-center-content-in-your-tickets-without-leaving-Agent-Workspace); [Knowledge in the context panel](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/7263163614874-Configuring-the-knowledge-section-in-the-context-panel)
- Intercom content-gap recommendations require the Pro add-on. They analyze failed Fin replies (escalations or poor answers) against successful human replies, teammate-handled replies, duplicates, and contradictions. Actions are add article or snippet, edit article or snippet, or review contradictory or duplicate content across articles, snippets, and webpages. The reviewer sees an impact score, the source conversations, and a red/green diff. Snippets accepted are available to Fin immediately. Article edits can be saved as a draft or published. Recommendations can be moved onto a different article. Create/edit suggestions run daily or weekly when volume, a topic that recurs for 7 days, or a 4-day spike qualifies. Duplicate and contradiction scans run Sundays, up to about 20 items. Pending reviews expire after 4 weeks. Low-volume workspaces may get few or no recommendations. Recommendations are not generated for conversations with no teammate reply, abandoned chats, cases where the human repeated Fin, feature requests, or bug reports. Permission required: “Can create and manage content in Knowledge.” — [Use AI-powered content recommendations](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/11394959-use-ai-powered-content-recommendations-to-improve-fin) (updated 2 Oct 2026)
- The same article does not say accepting a suggestion resolves, tags, or closes the conversations that triggered it. The output is a content change. Fin’s optimize guidance also says a teammate who sees a bad answer can open the source and update it, or write a snippet, without leaving the inbox. — [Top ten ways to optimize Fin](https://fin.ai/help/en/articles/13976013-top-ten-ways-to-optimize-fin) (12 Mar 2026)
- Intercom answer debugging shows up to 10 content items Fin treated as relevant and lets a teammate edit the article, snippet, or external content. The “Improve answer” button applies to AI answers generated after 15 Jul 2023. — [Debug Fin answers](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/8403222-debug-fin-ai-agent-answers)
- Help Scout’s documented loop is manual. Review AI Answers sessions, then decide whether the fix is an Improvement, a Guardrail, or a Docs edit. Article ratings feed the Docs report. Nothing in the fetched articles drafts a Docs article from a ticket or from negative feedback, or links that draft back to the conversation. — [Write Docs that work with AI Answers](https://docs.helpscout.com/article/1762-write-docs-that-work-well-with-ai-answers) (updated 2 Apr 2026); [Docs article ratings](https://docs.helpscout.com/article/1293-docs-article-ratings)
- Document360 lists feedback analytics, search analytics, Eddy analytics, and 404 analytics on the pricing grid, and the chatbot page says unanswered questions are tracked so content can be improved. No page fetched describes turning a ticket cluster or an unanswered question into a reviewed draft. Ticket deflection is a named feature (“Support Ticket Deflector”) and the chatbot can open a Zendesk or Freshdesk ticket. That is analytics plus escalation, not a docs change. — [Document360 pricing](https://document360.com/pricing/); [Eddy AI chatbot](https://document360.com/ai/chat-bot/)

### Inferences
- Intercom’s Pro recommendations are the closest shipped match to FrontLit’s “grouped issue becomes a draft a human reviews.” Differences that matter: it is plan-gated; snippets go live without a draft; the source material includes human replies and private snippets, not only published docs; there is no GitHub proposal; the original conversation is not part of the publish action.
- Zendesk’s knowledge builder is a one-shot bootstrap, and Zendesk says so. It is not the ongoing miss → evidence → draft loop FrontLit implemented. Generative-search feedback trains the ranker, which is analytics, not a content change.
- Help Scout and Document360 leave the loop at “look at ratings, sessions, and unanswered queries, then edit the article yourself.” That is the same class of product as a docs analytics tab, not a closed authoring loop.
- None of these systems keep the ticket open by policy when a draft is created, because they do not model that action. FrontLit’s explicit “draft does not auto-resolve” rule has no equivalent to compare against, except that Intercom’s accept action is also not described as resolving the customer.
- No product here documents autonomous customer replies as the default knowledge loop. Fin does reply autonomously and bills the outcome; that is the opposite of FrontLit’s “autopilot is later.” Zendesk AI agents also resolve without a human and bill for it. Help Scout AI Answers does the same at US$0.75. Document360’s chatbot is marketed as tier-1 deflection with human escalation. So the customer-facing autopilot these products already sell is exactly the scope FrontLit deferred.

### Gaps
- No Zendesk 2026 document was found for an ongoing “suggest an update to this existing article from these tickets” workflow. The customer comments say it is missing; Zendesk has not, in the fetched pages, confirmed a replacement.
- Help Scout Improvements are described as a way to add facts that are not in Docs. The article text does not say whether an Improvement is reviewed, published, or invisible to the help center. That is a small authoring surface sitting beside Docs, not a draft of a Docs article.
- Document360 ticket-deflector analytics (which searches, which articles, whether a “was this helpful” vote creates a task) were not opened. The feature is named on the pricing page only.

# What support suites add, and what they do not

### Takeaway
The support suites sell the operational layer a docs platform does not: shared inbox, macros, routing, SLAs on higher plans, and an agent copilot. In exchange they host the help center apart from the marketing site and do not use Git pull requests as the publish gate. Document360 is the outlier: it is the docs product, with embed and SEO closer to a docs platform, and it bolts on a chatbot and a ticket deflector instead of owning the inbox.

### Cited Findings
- Zendesk Support Team includes email ticketing, pre-written responses, routing, automations, triggers, and customer context. Higher Suite cards add messaging, chat, telephony, IVR, skills-based routing, and the AI agent and copilot split described above. Custom agent roles and approval workflows for content are on the sales-only enterprise card. — [Zendesk pricing](https://www.zendesk.com/pricing/)
- Intercom Essential includes Messenger, a shared inbox, ticketing, prebuilt reports, and a public help center, plus Fin. Advanced adds multiple inboxes, a workflow builder, and round-robin. Expert adds SLAs, SSO, HIPAA, and multibrand help center. WhatsApp, SMS, email campaigns, and phone are usage-priced. Macros are a Copilot source, not a Fin source and not a help-center source. — [Intercom pricing](https://www.intercom.com/pricing); [Knowledge sources](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/9440354-knowledge-sources-to-power-ai-agents-and-self-serve-support)
- Help Scout is a shared inbox plus Beacon plus Docs. AI Drafts draft a reply from past conversations and Docs but do not send it. Extra inboxes are US$10 per month yearly (or US$12 monthly) beyond the plan’s included count, from the pricing-page FAQ captured in search. — [Help Scout pricing](https://www.helpscout.com/pricing/); [AI Drafts](https://docs.helpscout.com/article/1570-ai-drafts)
- Document360 integrates with Zendesk, Freshdesk, Intercom, Slack, Teams, and names GitHub in the integration list, but the knowledge base is still the Document360 project. The embedded help center and the in-app widget are the documented ways to put that knowledge on another site. The chatbot escalates into Zendesk or Freshdesk rather than replacing them. — [Document360 pricing](https://document360.com/pricing/); [Eddy AI chatbot](https://document360.com/ai/chat-bot/)
- Help Scout cannot mount Docs at `domain.com/help` or on the apex domain. — [Custom domain](https://docs.helpscout.com/article/42-setup-custom-domain)
- No fetched page for any of the four describes Git as the source of truth, a pull-request review, or excluding unmerged branches from retrieval. Intercom Fin Releases (beta, help article updated 29 Sep 2026) batch help-center and Fin config changes in a release workspace and publish them together. If two releases touch the same item, the published release replaces the live version; they are not merged. Rollback is not supported for snippets, internal articles, escalation guidance, or deleted items. That is a release bundle, not Git. — [Releases for Fin](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/16041751-releases-for-fin-beta)

### Inferences
- FrontLit’s split, where docs-only members cannot see raw tickets, is the reverse of these suites. Fin, Zendesk AI agents, Help Scout AI Drafts, and the Document360 chatbot all treat tickets or past conversations as a knowledge source for at least one assistant. Intercom is the only one that draws a hard line: conversation history and macros feed Copilot, not Fin. That is the nearest equivalent to “do not answer customers from raw tickets,” and it is a setting, not a permission boundary for human authors.
- Multi-brand help centers are an Intercom Expert feature and a Help Scout multi-site feature (paid extra sites). Zendesk’s current public cards do not state the count. FrontLit’s single `/docs` on the team site is a deliberate non-feature relative to all three suites.
- Marketing-site embedding is the docs-platform feature these support KBs lack, with two partial exceptions: Document360’s embedded help center, and Help Scout’s API if you rebuild the site yourself. Neither is “the article is a page in the marketing CMS, rendered with the site theme.”

### Gaps
- Current Zendesk SLA plan gate was not on the fetched pricing cards. The Jan 2021 Suite article lists SLAs on Professional; that is stale.
- Help Scout SLA plan placement was only in a secondary review, so it is not recorded as fact.
- No source described a docs-only role that is barred from tickets in Zendesk, Intercom, or Help Scout. Document360 is not the system of record for tickets, so the question does not arise the same way.

# Notion and Confluence as public docs

### Takeaway
Both are used as public documents in 2026, and Atlassian’s own docs list “support documentation” and “an open knowledge base” as reasons to turn on anonymous access. They are still wikis. Notion Sites can be indexed and can use a paid custom domain, but publishing is per page tree, SEO is limited, and there is no help-center citation or ticket loop. Confluence anonymous spaces are indexable by default once public; public links are a paid, per-item share and are not a help center.

### Cited Findings
- Notion Sites can publish unlimited pages. The free plan includes one `notion.site` domain and a search-engine indexing toggle. Paid plans include up to five `notion.site` domains, a homepage, site customization, Google Analytics, SEO link title and description, and custom slugs up to 60 characters. Indexing can take up to four weeks. Publishing a page publishes its subpages unless those subpages are restricted. The site updates as the Notion page is edited. There is no password protection. Custom domains are an add-on for paid plans only: US$10 per domain per month, or US$8 per month on annual billing, up to 25 domains, billed on the same cycle as Notion. Enterprise workspace owners can disable publishing of sites, forms, and public links, which unpublishes sites already live. Published pages expose contributor names, profile photos, and email addresses in page metadata. — [Publish a Notion Site](https://www.notion.com/help/public-pages-and-web-publishing); [Notion Sites availability and pricing](https://www.notion.com/help/notion-sites-availability-and-pricing)
- Notion Sites can be embedded in another website by copying an embed snippet. That is an embed of the Notion page, not a themed docs section. — [Publish a Notion Site](https://www.notion.com/help/public-pages-and-web-publishing)
- Confluence Cloud anonymous access makes a space available to anyone on the internet. Atlassian says it is all or nothing, and that if anonymous users can access the site, the content is indexed by search engines and can appear in Google. Documented uses include a public roadmap, an open knowledge base, and support documentation. Access is controlled at site, space, and content level. Anonymous users see the whole space unless an item or a parent is restricted. New sites require anonymous access to be enabled at the site level; older sites may still let space admins grant it independently until “enforce site-level control” is turned on. The feature is unavailable in Atlassian Government Cloud. — [Make a space public](https://support.atlassian.com/confluence-cloud/docs/make-a-space-public/)
- Confluence public links, on Standard, Premium, and Enterprise only, share one page, live doc, or whiteboard. They ignore parent, space, and site view restrictions. Child pages are not included. Visitors get a view-only page that hides the content tree and most Confluence functions. Blogs and databases cannot have public links. The public-links article fetched on 2026-10-10 does not mention search indexing. — [Share content externally with public links](https://support.atlassian.com/confluence-cloud/docs/share-content-externally-with-public-links/)
- A 23 Apr 2026 Atlassian Community post by an app vendor claims Atlassian’s own docs say public links are not indexed by search engines. That sentence was not present on the official public-links page fetched for this note, so it is unverified. — [Atlassian Community, 23 Apr 2026](https://community.atlassian.com/forums/App-Central-articles/Turn-Confluence-Documentation-Into-a-Channel-for-New-Customers/ba-p/3225593)
- Intercom’s source matrix syncs Notion and Confluence in as internal articles for Fin and Copilot, not as help-center articles. So both wikis are already used as private answer sources for a support bot, which is a different job from publishing them as the customer help center. — [Knowledge sources](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/9440354-knowledge-sources-to-power-ai-agents-and-self-serve-support)

### Inferences
- The usual gap versus FrontLit, and versus Zendesk, Intercom, Help Scout, or Document360, is the help-center surface: no article-level publish permission separate from “the page is public,” no documented passage citations for a customer bot, no ticket handoff, no “was this helpful,” and no retrieval filter that excludes drafts. Notion’s publish-the-children default and the metadata leak of contributor emails are the opposite of a curated docs corpus.
- SEO is the other gap. Notion requires a paid plan for a custom title and description, indexing can take four weeks, and the free URL is `notion.site`. Confluence anonymous spaces are indexed as Confluence pages, with no per-article meta description or sitemap control documented on the pages fetched. Help Scout and Document360 both generate sitemaps and expose meta fields; Notion and Confluence do not match that.
- Git is absent. Notion Sites update live from the page. Confluence anonymous pages are the live space. Neither has a PR review gate.
- They are still used as public docs because the editor is already where the team writes. The 2026 vendor docs support that use. They do not replace a help center that has to ground a chatbot and a copilot on the same published passages.

### Gaps
- No official Confluence page was fetched for a custom domain, per-page meta description, or sitemap on an anonymous space.
- Notion has no documented support-bot citation feature. Third-party “Notion help center” products exist; they were not in scope beyond noting that teams add them because Notion Sites do not provide search-quality help-center behavior.
- Confluence Rovo can answer inside Confluence. Whether those answers cite pages for anonymous visitors was not researched. It is an internal writing and search assistant on the pages seen, not a customer help-center bot.
